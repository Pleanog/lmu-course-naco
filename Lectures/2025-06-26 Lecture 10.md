### **Local vs. Global Information**  
Optimization algorithms differ in whether they use local or global information.  
Gradient Descent relies solely on local gradient information and thus typically moves within a local neighborhood — often converging to a local optimum. In contrast, Random Search ignores any local information and explores the entire solution landscape randomly.  
Depending on the problem structure, this choice can be crucial: local strategies are efficient in smooth, convex spaces but may fail in more complex landscapes.

### **Smoothness**  
The term _smoothness_ describes how much the objective function changes with small changes in the input values.  
In a smooth landscape (e.g., convex or continuously differentiable), gradient-based methods like Gradient Descent work very well.  
However, if the landscape is rugged or "jagged," the local gradient may point in an unfavorable direction or change abruptly — reducing the effectiveness of such methods.  
Therefore, the choice of algorithm should take the nature of the optimization space into account.

### **Deception**  
A problem is _non-deceptive_ if the local gradient always points toward the global optimum.  
Deceptive structures, on the other hand, mislead the algorithm: a classic example is a well on top of a hill — from the outside, all gradients point away from the global minimum (inside the well). Gradient-based methods would hardly find this point.  
Such deceptions pose a challenge for local search methods and require robust strategies such as Simulated Annealing or population-based search.

### **Exploration vs. Exploitation**  
This fundamental dilemma describes the trade-off between:

- **Exploration** (searching new, unknown regions of the search space)
- **Exploitation** (refining already discovered, promising solutions)

Gradient-based methods tend to focus on exploitation, while stochastic approaches like Simulated Annealing allow for more exploration in early stages (e.g., via "high temperature").  
A well-designed algorithm must balance both aspects to efficiently and reliably find the globally best solution.
